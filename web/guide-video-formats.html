A beginner's guide to video formats

Updated October 2026 · 6 minute read

Choosing a video format is mostly about compatibility, file size and what you plan to do with the file. You usually do not need the most technically advanced format; you need the one that works reliably for your audience.

MP4

MP4 is the safest general-purpose choice. It is widely supported by browsers, phones, computers and social platforms. H.264 video with AAC audio remains a practical compatibility combination.

WebM

WebM is designed for web delivery and commonly uses VP9 or AV1 video. It can be efficient, but compatibility with some editing software and older devices is less universal than MP4.

MOV

MOV is common in Apple and professional editing workflows. It may contain high-quality codecs and can produce very large files.

GIF

GIF is useful for short, silent animations, not general video. It has limited color depth and often creates much larger files than modern video codecs for the same visual duration.

What should you choose?

  • Choose MP4 when compatibility matters most.
  • Choose WebM for web-focused workflows where modern browser support is acceptable.
  • Keep MOV when it is part of your editing workflow.
  • Use GIF only for short animations that specifically need GIF compatibility.
